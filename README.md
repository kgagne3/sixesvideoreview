# Sixes Video Review (SVR)

**One game, called around the world. Let's see it together.**

Live site: **https://kgagne3.github.io/sixesvideoreview**

---

## Why this exists

Sixes is one sport with one rulebook, but it is officiated in many places. A slash in Canada, a
cross-check in Germany, a hold in Puerto Rico, a push in Australia, the Philippines or Chinese
Taipei: each crew brings its own training, its own habits and its own sense of what matters. Those
differences are normal. They are also hard to see from inside one region, and harder still to talk
about when all we have is words.

Sixes Video Review puts the calls themselves side by side. Officials don't have to describe how
something is called "back home". They can open the clip and show it. Video takes the guesswork,
and the defensiveness, out of the conversation and leaves the play.

SVR is an **identification tool**. It finds where we line up and where we drift apart: by
discipline, between continental qualifiers, and across regions of the world. Once those patterns
are visible, we can work on them together through video rather than debate. That raises the level
of development for every official and brings our global community into alignment.

## What it does

- **Every foul is its own clip.** Each clip is cut from the full game broadcast around the moment of
  the call, and comes with the violation, the card (green, yellow or red), the team, the game and the
  game clock.
- **Filter it however you want to learn.** Narrow by violation, card, event, gender, team or game,
  or search for anything. Pull up every dangerous propulsion from both qualifiers, every red card
  in the women's games, or every call against a single team.
- **Compare across regions.** The same violation from the Pan-American and the Asia-Pacific
  qualifiers sits in one list. Watch how a call is judged, signalled and carded in one part of the
  world, then compare it with another.
- **Context, one click away.** Every clip links back to the full game replay at the moment of the
  foul, so you can see what led up to it and what followed.
- **Built for the rink and the couch.** The site works on a phone between games and has a large
  theater mode for film sessions and crew meetings.
- **No sign-in and nothing to install.** Share the link and anyone can start watching.

## What's in SVR1.0

**301 foul clips** from two 2026 World Lacrosse continental sixes qualifiers:

| Event | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 Pan-American Sixes Lacrosse Championships (PALA) | 25 | 115 |
| 2026 Asia-Pacific Sixes Lacrosse Championships (APLU) | 86 | 75 |

- **20 nations** on the floor: Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, Chile, China, Chinese Taipei,
  Haudenosaunee, Hong Kong (China), India, Jamaica, Japan, Korea, Mexico, New Zealand, the
  Philippines, Puerto Rico, Saudi Arabia, Singapore and the USA.
- **19 violation types**, from the everyday (slashing, interference, walling, cross-checks) to the
  rare and serious (dangerous propulsion, dangerous follow-through, illegal body checks, expulsion
  fouls).
- **200 yellow, 86 green and 15 red cards.**

Clips are also organized into playlists on the BCSportsTV YouTube channel, by event and by violation.

## How to use it

- **Crew and pre-game prep:** pull up the violations you expect to see and agree on the standard
  before the first faceoff.
- **Clinics and mentoring:** build a session around one violation and watch it called across
  several countries and both qualifiers.
- **Self-review:** check your own instincts against the way other regions call the same play.
- **Cross-region conversations:** send a clip, not a paragraph. "This one, at 6:18" settles more
  than an hour of description.

## Where it's going

SVR1.0 covers the Pan-American and Asia-Pacific qualifiers. The goal is to grow it into a truly
global library: more events, more regions (Europe and beyond) and more disciplines. Then any
official, anywhere, can see how the game is being called everywhere else, and we can grow
together.

If you are an official, assignor or educator and want to contribute footage or ideas, get in touch
through BCSportsTV.
